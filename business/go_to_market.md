# Go-To-Market — Aquarium V0 / COLORBOOST

> Channels, positioning, and launch sequence.
> Audience: Indian ornamental-fish hobbyists.

---

## 1. Target customer

**Primary**: Indian aquarium hobbyist who keeps ornamental fish (Arowana,
Discus, Goldfish, Guppies, Bettas, Cichlids) and currently owns either
a generic Amazon LED or a TICA Color Up.

**Pain we solve**:
1. "My fish don't look as colourful as the photos online"
2. "My current light makes the water look red/blue/purple"
3. "The light broke after 8 months and I can't get it fixed"
4. "I have no idea what spectrum my light is"

**Not targeting**: planted-tank enthusiasts (different segment, different
price point). Planted-tank buyers already accept ₹10k+ pricing.

---

## 2. Positioning statement

> COLORBOOST is the only aquarium light in India that publishes its
> spectrum, runs cool without a fan, and can be repaired by the owner.
> Designed in India for Indian tanks, backed by measured data.

Three pillars:
1. **Measurable** — every unit ships with spectrum + thermal data
2. **Repairable** — open schematic, replaceable drivers, India-local sourcing
3. **Affordable premium** — better engineering than TICA, lower price

---

## 3. Channels

### Phase 1 — Direct D2C (start here, qty 1 → 100)
- **Own website** (Shopify / WooCommerce) — primary
  - Full-margin, full data, full brand control
  - ₹500–2,000/month ad spend on Instagram + Google
- **Instagram organic** — aquarium hobby community, before/after fish photos
- **YouTube** — short technical videos ("how the spectrum was measured")

**Cost to set up**: ₹30,000–50,000 (Shopify subscription, domain, photo shoot)

### Phase 2 — Marketplace (qty 100 → 500)
- **Amazon India** — high visibility, but 15% referral + ad spend
  - Use FBA (Fulfilled by Amazon) for shipping + returns handling
  - Budget ₹50,000–1,00,000/month for sponsored product ads
- **Flipkart** — smaller fish-keeping audience than Amazon, lower priority

**Reality check**: Amazon takes 25–30% all-in (referral + ads + shipping).
D2C always wins on margin if you can drive traffic.

### Phase 3 — Aquarium shop dealer network (qty 300+)
- India has ~3,000–5,000 aquarium shops; top 200 shops in metros sell
  premium equipment
- Offer 25% margin (dealer buys at ₹2,625 if retail is ₹3,500)
- Dealer kit: 1 demo unit, brochure, banner, training
- Target: 50 active dealers by qty 1,000

**Dealer acquisition**: visit in person. Email doesn't work for this segment.

### Phase 4 — B2B (qty 1000+)
- Hotel aquariums, restaurant displays, corporate lobbies
- Aquarium maintenance companies (they buy 10–50 units/year each)
- Lower priority but stable volume

---

## 4. Pricing strategy

### MSRP (manufacturer suggested retail)
- ₹3,500 for 60 cm V0
- Future: ₹3,000 for 30 cm, ₹4,500 for 90 cm

### Launch pricing (first 100 units)
- ₹2,999 "founder's price" — 15% off, customer commits to write a review
- Generates first 100 verified reviews on Amazon + website
- After 100 units: revert to ₹3,500

### Bundle ideas
- V0 + spare fuse pack: ₹3,700 (upsell)
- V0 + spare LED set: ₹4,000 (15% margin on spares)
- V0 + diffusers ×2 (clear + opal): ₹4,200 (margin play)

---

## 5. Launch sequence

### Pre-launch (2–3 months before V1)
- Build 5 demo units (V0 design)
- Photograph them on real fish tanks (Discus, Goldfish, Arowana if available)
- Write 5 detailed blog posts: "Why V0 uses 660 nm deep red", "How to
  measure your aquarium light's spectrum", "Repair vs replace: the
  COLORBOOST approach"
- Collect 5–10 emails of aquarium hobbyists for early-access list
- Set up Instagram + YouTube with consistent posting

### V0 → V1 transition
- V0 (qty 1) = engineering sample, internal only
- V1 alpha (qty 10) = sent to 10 selected reviewers in different cities
  - Conditions: written review + photos, return unit after 3 months
  - In exchange: free unit + ₹1,000 honorarium
- V1 small launch (qty 50) = open D2C sales at "founder's price"

### V2 (qty 500+)
- Amazon India launch with FBA
- Dealer network activation (top 30 cities)
- Sponsored YouTube videos with aquarium hobbyist influencers

### V3 (qty 1000+)
- Add 30 cm and 90 cm variants
- App launch (BLE control)
- BIS registration → can sell to commercial channels

## 6. Brand identity

### Name
- **Working name**: COLORBOOST
- **Backup**: ORNAMENTAL (matches "ornamental fish" + sounds premium)
- **Tagline options**:
  - "Light that makes fish look like fish."
  - "Engineered for colour. Built to last."
  - "Spectrum you can measure. Light you can repair."

### Visual identity
- Black + warm white + accent of red-orange (colour-enhancement theme)
- Photography style: macro of fish scales with clear scale definition
- Avoid: generic blue aquarium stock photos (overused by competitors)

### Voice
- Technical but accessible
- Indian English (e.g., "colour", not "color")
- Speak to the hobbyist, not the "customer"
- Show your data openly (spectrum charts, thermal data)

## 10. What NOT to do

- **Do not** chase every aquarium-shop dealer in the first 6 months —
  focus on D2C and 5–10 key dealers
- **Do not** discount below ₹3,000 — kills margin
- **Do not** launch 4 channel variants at once — ship 60 cm first, add
  30 / 90 cm at qty 500+
- **Do not** promise app features in V0 — defer to V1 firmware honestly
- **Do not** skip the spectrum data sheets — they're the moat

---

## 11. Why this can work

1. TICA's distribution is weak outside metros — D2C + Instagram covers
   tier-2 cities better
2. Indian aquarium hobbyists are unusually data-curious (YouTube
   influence is high) — the measured-spectrum angle resonates
3. TICA's repair story is zero — even a basic repair manual is a real
   differentiator
4. ₹3,500 is a reachable price point for a serious hobbyist (most
   spend ₹5,000+ on fish alone)
5. The Indian PCB supply chain is mature enough to hit <₹2,000 BOM at
   qty 1,000 — the economics work
